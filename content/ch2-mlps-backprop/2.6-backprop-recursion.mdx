## 1. Why this session exists

Once we have $\delta^{(L)} = \hat y - y$ at the output (2.5), we need a way to push that gradient signal *backwards* — to $\delta^{(L-1)}$, then $\delta^{(L-2)}$, all the way to $\delta^{(1)}$. The chain rule from 0.3 gives us exactly one recursion, with two factors at each step: a matrix multiply by $W^{(\ell+1)}$ and an elementwise multiply by $g'^{(\ell)}(z^{(\ell)})$. This session derives it.

---

## 2. Setup

Recall the forward pass at consecutive layers:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z^{(\ell)}   &= W^{(\ell)} a^{(\ell-1)} + b^{(\ell)}, \\
a^{(\ell)}   &= g^{(\ell)}(z^{(\ell)}), \\
z^{(\ell+1)} &= W^{(\ell+1)} a^{(\ell)} + b^{(\ell+1)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The dependency graph at this step is

$$
z^{(\ell)} \;\xrightarrow{g^{(\ell)}}\; a^{(\ell)} \;\xrightarrow{W^{(\ell+1)},\, +\, b^{(\ell+1)}}\; z^{(\ell+1)}.
$$

The loss $L$ depends on $z^{(\ell)}$ only through $z^{(\ell+1)}$ (via $a^{(\ell)}$). By the chain rule,

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial z^{(\ell)}} \;=\; \frac{\partial L}{\partial z^{(\ell+1)}} \cdot \frac{\partial z^{(\ell+1)}}{\partial a^{(\ell)}} \cdot \frac{\partial a^{(\ell)}}{\partial z^{(\ell)}}.
$$

Each Jacobian factor is easy.

---

## 3. Each factor in turn

**$\partial z^{(\ell+1)} / \partial a^{(\ell)} = W^{(\ell+1)}$.** Direct from the affine forward $z^{(\ell+1)} = W^{(\ell+1)} a^{(\ell)} + b^{(\ell+1)}$. In the numerator convention this Jacobian is the $d_{\ell+1} \times d_\ell$ matrix $W^{(\ell+1)}$ itself.

**$\partial a^{(\ell)} / \partial z^{(\ell)} = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(g'^{(\ell)}(z^{(\ell)})\bigr)$.** The activation $g^{(\ell)}$ is applied elementwise, so each $a^{(\ell)}_i$ depends only on $z^{(\ell)}_i$. The Jacobian is diagonal with $i$-th entry $g'(z^{(\ell)}_i)$.

**$\partial L / \partial z^{(\ell+1)} = (\delta^{(\ell+1)})^\top$** by definition of the next-layer delta — written as a row in the numerator convention.

---

## 4. Putting it together

Substituting,

$$
\bigl(\delta^{(\ell)}\bigr)^\top \;=\; (\delta^{(\ell+1)})^\top \cdot W^{(\ell+1)} \cdot \operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(g'^{(\ell)}(z^{(\ell)})\bigr).
$$

Transpose both sides to get a column:

$$
\delta^{(\ell)} \;=\; \operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(g'^{(\ell)}(z^{(\ell)})\bigr) \cdot (W^{(\ell+1)})^\top \cdot \delta^{(\ell+1)}.
$$

Multiplying a vector by $\operatorname{diag}(g'(z))$ is exactly elementwise multiplication by $g'(z)$. So

$$
\boxed{\ \delta^{(\ell)} \;=\; \Bigl((W^{(\ell+1)})^\top\, \delta^{(\ell+1)}\Bigr) \,\odot\, g'^{(\ell)}\bigl(z^{(\ell)}\bigr) \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}.\ }
$$

This is the **backprop recursion**. Two factors per step:

1. **Matrix-multiply step.** Multiply $\delta^{(\ell+1)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\ell+1}}$ by $(W^{(\ell+1)})^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell \times d_{\ell+1}}$. This "spreads" the gradient signal across layer-$\ell$ units in proportion to how strongly each contributed to layer $\ell+1$. Cost $\Theta(d_\ell\, d_{\ell+1})$ multiply-adds.
2. **Activation step.** Elementwise multiply by $g'^{(\ell)}(z^{(\ell)}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}$. This "scales" the gradient by how responsive each unit was at its current operating point. Cost $\Theta(d_\ell)$ multiplies.

Total cost per backward step: dominated by the matmul — same order as the forward.

---

## 5. Sanity check: dimensions

- $W^{(\ell+1)}$ is $d_{\ell+1} \times d_\ell$, so $(W^{(\ell+1)})^\top$ is $d_\ell \times d_{\ell+1}$.
- $\delta^{(\ell+1)}$ is $d_{\ell+1}$, so $(W^{(\ell+1)})^\top \delta^{(\ell+1)}$ is $d_\ell$ ✓.
- $g'^{(\ell)}(z^{(\ell)})$ is $d_\ell$, so the Hadamard product is $d_\ell$ ✓.
- $\delta^{(\ell)}$ is $d_\ell$ ✓.

<Aside type="history">
The original backpropagation paper (Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams, 1986, *Nature*) derives the same recursion in "scalars only" indexed form, $\partial E / \partial w^{(j)}_{il}$. The matrix form used here is the standard modern convention; it is identical content, just easier to vectorise on hardware.
</Aside>

---

## 6. Batched form

Define $\Delta^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}$ (rows = examples). The recursion becomes

$$
\boxed{\ \Delta^{(\ell)} \;=\; \bigl(\Delta^{(\ell+1)}\, W^{(\ell+1)}\bigr) \,\odot\, g'^{(\ell)}\!\bigl(Z^{(\ell)}\bigr) \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}.\ }
$$

Worked dimensions:

- $\Delta^{(\ell+1)}$ is $B \times d_{\ell+1}$.
- $W^{(\ell+1)}$ is $d_{\ell+1} \times d_\ell$.
- $\Delta^{(\ell+1)} W^{(\ell+1)}$ is $B \times d_\ell$ ✓.
- $g'(Z^{(\ell)})$ is $B \times d_\ell$ ✓.
- Hadamard product is $B \times d_\ell$ ✓.

The *single-example* form has $(W^{(\ell+1)})^\top \delta$, but the *batched row-major* form has $\Delta\, W^{(\ell+1)}$ without a transpose — this is the row-vs-column distinction. Keep one convention and stick to it.

---

## 7. Why this is efficient

The forward pass at layer $\ell$ does one matrix-multiply $A W^\top$ of cost $\Theta(B\, d_\ell\, d_{\ell-1})$.

The backward pass at layer $\ell$ does one matrix-multiply $\Delta W$ of cost $\Theta(B\, d_\ell\, d_{\ell+1})$ plus one elementwise op of cost $\Theta(B\, d_\ell)$.

**Backward = forward in cost.** This is the fundamental reason gradient descent on deep nets is practical: the price of one gradient computation is one extra forward-pass-equivalent of FLOPs. Forward + backward + parameter update ≈ 3× one forward pass. Without the trick — say, computing $\partial L / \partial \theta$ via $N$ finite-difference forward passes — we'd be paying $N \times$ forward. For $N \sim 10^9$ parameters, that is the difference between hours and millennia.

---

## 8. Takeaways

- $\delta^{(\ell)} = ((W^{(\ell+1)})^\top \delta^{(\ell+1)}) \odot g'^{(\ell)}(z^{(\ell)})$ — the central recursion.
- Batched: $\Delta^{(\ell)} = (\Delta^{(\ell+1)} W^{(\ell+1)}) \odot g'(Z^{(\ell)})$.
- Cost per layer = one matmul, same scale as the forward pass.
- This is why deep learning is computationally feasible.
