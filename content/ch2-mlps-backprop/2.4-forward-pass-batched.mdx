## 1. Why this session exists

In code, MLPs process a mini-batch of $B$ examples at a time, not one example at a time — that is what makes GPU computation efficient. The math is identical, but the shapes change. Getting layer shapes right *cold* (no derivation, you see "$B \times d_2$" and just know what's there) is a skill we want before we start computing gradients in 2.5–2.7.

---

## 2. Single example: refresher

From 2.1, per-example forward at layer $\ell$:

$$
z^{(\ell)} = W^{(\ell)} a^{(\ell-1)} + b^{(\ell)}, \qquad a^{(\ell)} = g^{(\ell)}(z^{(\ell)}),
$$

with $a^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}$, $W^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}}$, $b^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}$.

---

## 3. Batch: stacking examples as rows

Let $A^{(\ell-1)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_{\ell-1}}$ be the batch of activations at layer $\ell - 1$, one example per row. Then the batched pre-activation is

$$
\boxed{\ Z^{(\ell)} \;=\; A^{(\ell-1)}\, (W^{(\ell)})^\top \;+\; \mathbf{1}_B\, (b^{(\ell)})^\top \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell},\ }
$$

with $\mathbf{1}_B$ the all-ones column. In NumPy/PyTorch, the bias addition is implemented by broadcasting: you just write `Z = A @ W.T + b`.

The batched activation is the pointwise nonlinearity applied entry-by-entry:

$$
A^{(\ell)} = g^{(\ell)}(Z^{(\ell)}) \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}.
$$

**Why the transpose?** Because we chose to put examples as **rows** of $A$. The matrix-vector product $W^{(\ell)} a$ from §2 has $a$ as a column on the right; to apply the same map to every row of $A$, the convenient form is $A\, W^\top$.

<Aside type="note">
The "examples as rows" convention is what NumPy / PyTorch use by default. An equivalent column-major convention puts examples as columns; then the formula is $Z = W A + b\, \mathbf{1}^\top$ with no transpose. Pick one and stick with it; we'll stay row-major for the rest of the course.
</Aside>

---

## 4. Dimension table

Every shape you'll ever need at the forward pass.

- Input batch: $A^{(0)} = X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_0}$.
- Weights: $W^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}}$ (output dim × input dim).
- Bias: $b^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}$ (output dim).
- Pre-activation: $Z^{(\ell)} = A^{(\ell-1)} (W^{(\ell)})^\top + b^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}$.
- Activation: $A^{(\ell)} = g^{(\ell)}(Z^{(\ell)}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}$.
- Output logits: $Z^{(L)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_L}$.
- Output probabilities: $\hat Y = \operatorname{softmax}(Z^{(L)},\ \text{axis} = 1) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_L}$ — softmax over the class axis, separately for each row.

A worked instance — MNIST with $(d_0, d_1, d_2, d_3) = (784, 256, 128, 10)$ and batch $B = 32$:

- $X$ is $32 \times 784$.
- $W^{(1)}$ is $256 \times 784$, $b^{(1)}$ is $256$.
- $Z^{(1)}$ is $32 \times 256$, $A^{(1)}$ is $32 \times 256$.
- $W^{(2)}$ is $128 \times 256$, $b^{(2)}$ is $128$.
- $Z^{(2)}$ is $32 \times 128$, $A^{(2)}$ is $32 \times 128$.
- $W^{(3)}$ is $10 \times 128$, $b^{(3)}$ is $10$.
- $Z^{(3)}$ is $32 \times 10$, $\hat Y$ is $32 \times 10$.

If you can write this table cold, the rest of the chapter follows mechanically.

## 5. The training loss in batch form

For multi-class classification with $K = d_L$ classes and one-hot targets $Y \in \{0, 1\}^{B \times K}$,

$$
L(\theta) \;=\; -\frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \sum_{k=1}^K Y_{i, k}\, \log \hat Y_{i, k},
$$

equivalently $-\frac{1}{B} \operatorname{tr}(Y^\top \log \hat Y)$ with $\log$ elementwise. Using the log-sum-exp form from 0.10 / 1.7,

$$
L(\theta) \;=\; \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \Bigl[\, -z^{(L)}_{i, y_i} \;+\; \log \sum_{j=1}^K e^{\,z^{(L)}_{i, j}} \,\Bigr],
$$

i.e. per-row stable log-sum-exp plus per-row true-class logit subtraction, then average. In PyTorch this is `F.cross_entropy(logits, y)` with `logits` of shape $B \times K$ and `y` of shape $B$ (class indices).

---

## 6. Why this matters for what's next

Backprop in 2.5–2.7 will be derived in the same batched matrix form. Knowing the shapes ahead of time prevents silly transposition bugs: each gradient should be the same shape as its parameter, every $\delta^{(\ell)}$ should be the same shape as $Z^{(\ell)}$, and any product that isn't $d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}$ where we wanted weights is a sign we got something wrong.

---

## 7. Takeaways

- Examples-as-rows: $A^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}$, $W^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}}$, $Z^{(\ell)} = A^{(\ell-1)} (W^{(\ell)})^\top + b^{(\ell)}$.
- Bias is broadcast across the batch axis.
- The forward pass is $L$ matrix-multiplies and $L$ pointwise activations.
- Loss is a scalar summary; we'll backprop *from* it through this graph in 2.5.
