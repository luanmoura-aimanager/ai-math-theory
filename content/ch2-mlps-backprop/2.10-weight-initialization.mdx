## 1. Why this session exists

Section 2.9 left an open question: even if the activation $g$ has well-behaved $g'$, the matrix factors $(W^{(\ell+1)})^\top$ in the backprop chain can themselves rescale the gradient. To keep activations *and* gradients well-scaled across depth, we initialise weights with a carefully chosen variance. The two recipes are **Xavier/Glorot** (linear or tanh) and **He** (ReLU).

This is one of those moments where deep learning's empirical fix turns out to come from a one-line calculation.

---

## 2. Setup: variance propagation through a linear layer

Consider one linear layer

$$
z = W a + b
$$

with $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$, $a \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{in}}}$, $b = 0$ (for now). Assume entries of $W$ are i.i.d. with mean $0$ and variance $\sigma_W^2$, independent of $a$. Then

$$
z_i = \sum_{j=1}^{d_{\text{in}}} W_{i, j}\, a_j.
$$

Compute $\operatorname{Var}(z_i)$. With $\mathbb{E}[W_{i, j}] = 0$ and independence,

$$
\operatorname{Var}(z_i) \;=\; \sum_j \operatorname{Var}(W_{i, j}\, a_j) \;=\; \sum_j \mathbb{E}[W_{i, j}^2]\, \mathbb{E}[a_j^2] \;=\; d_{\text{in}} \cdot \sigma_W^2 \cdot \operatorname{Var}(a).
$$

(With $a_j$ centred and i.i.d. across $j$, $\mathbb{E}[a_j^2] = \operatorname{Var}(a)$.)

**To preserve variance** through the layer, $\operatorname{Var}(z) = \operatorname{Var}(a)$, we need

$$
\boxed{\ \sigma_W^2 \;=\; \frac{1}{d_{\text{in}}}.\ }
$$

This is the **variance-preservation principle** for forward propagation.

---

## 3. Xavier / Glorot initialisation (Glorot & Bengio, 2010)<FN n={1} />

Glorot's full analysis does the same calculation in the backward pass and asks for *both* forward and backward variances to be preserved. Backward through the linear layer (from 2.6),

$$
\operatorname{Var}\bigl((W^\top \delta)_i\bigr) = d_{\text{out}} \cdot \sigma_W^2 \cdot \operatorname{Var}(\delta).
$$

To preserve $\operatorname{Var}(\delta)$ we need $\sigma_W^2 = 1 / d_{\text{out}}$. Forward wants $1 / d_{\text{in}}$; backward wants $1 / d_{\text{out}}$. Compromise: take the harmonic mean,

$$
\boxed{\ \sigma_W^2 \;=\; \frac{2}{d_{\text{in}} + d_{\text{out}}} \quad\text{(Xavier / Glorot)}.\ }
$$

In practice we sample $W_{i, j} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_W^2)$ or, equivalently variance-wise, $W_{i, j} \sim \mathcal{U}(-r, r)$ with $r = \sqrt{6 / (d_{\text{in}} + d_{\text{out}})}$.

This was the right choice when the dominant activation was tanh, which has $\tanh'(0) = 1$ — so the activation doesn't change the variance budget. With sigmoid hidden layers, Xavier was already a marked improvement on the previous "random scale, hope" approach.

---

## 4. He initialisation (He et al., 2015)<FN n={2} />

For ReLU activations the calculation needs one more step. Recall (2.3, 2.9) that $\operatorname{ReLU}'(z) \in \{0, 1\}$ and that for a symmetrically distributed $z$, $\operatorname{ReLU}(z) = 0$ half the time. Plugging this into the variance propagation:

$$
\operatorname{Var}\bigl(\operatorname{ReLU}(z)\bigr) = \tfrac{1}{2}\, \operatorname{Var}(z),
$$

assuming a centred symmetric $z$. So *passing through a ReLU halves the variance*. To compensate, the next layer's weight matrix should *double* the variance budget:

$$
\boxed{\ \sigma_W^2 \;=\; \frac{2}{d_{\text{in}}} \quad\text{(He / Kaiming, for ReLU)}.\ }
$$

This is the **He** (or **Kaiming**) initialisation — the default for ReLU networks today (`nn.init.kaiming_normal_` in PyTorch).

**Why this matters concretely.** Without the $2 / d_{\text{in}}$ correction, ReLU networks see their activation variance halve at every layer; after $L = 20$ layers, the typical activation magnitude has shrunk by $2^{-10} \approx 10^{-3}$, and the network is effectively dead. He init keeps the activation variance constant across depth.

---

## 5. Generalisations

Two natural extensions.

**Generic activation $g$ with derivative-magnitude $\eta := \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[(g'(Z))^2]}$ at the initialisation scale.** The compensating variance is

$$
\sigma_W^2 = \frac{1}{\eta^2 \cdot d_{\text{in}}}.
$$

Plugging $\eta^2 = 1/2$ (ReLU half-on) gives $2 / d_{\text{in}}$, recovering He. Plugging $\eta = 1$ (tanh near zero) gives $1 / d_{\text{in}}$, recovering Xavier-forward.

**Layer-wise scale rescue.** Modern alternatives like **LSUV** (Mishkin & Matas, 2016) iteratively rescale each layer's weights so that the actual variance of $z^{(\ell)}$ at initialisation matches a target value, measured on a real batch. More accurate than the closed-form rule, more annoying to implement.

---

## 6. Biases

Biases are usually initialised to zero. There is no variance to preserve — biases shift means, not variances. Exception: the bias of the *output* logit for a binary classifier on imbalanced data is sometimes initialised to $\operatorname{logit}(\mathbb{P}(y = 1))$ so that the network's prior prediction at step 0 matches the class frequency.

---

## 7. A common pitfall

Many older codebases (and CV pipelines from the early 2010s) use the forward-only Xavier variance $\operatorname{Var}(w^{(\ell)}) = 1 / d_{\ell - 1}$ across the board — tanh or ReLU, indifferently. For tanh-activated networks this is fine. For ReLU networks it under-scales the weights by a factor of $2$ in variance, and the activation variance halves at every layer (§4). The fix is one line: switch the scaling factor from $1/d_{\text{in}}$ to $2/d_{\text{in}}$ (or call `nn.init.kaiming_normal_` in PyTorch) and trainability improves immediately. A small but real example of how the theory keeps catching up to the practice.

---

## 8. Takeaways

- Forward-variance preservation: $\sigma_W^2 = 1 / d_{\text{in}}$.
- Backward-variance preservation: $\sigma_W^2 = 1 / d_{\text{out}}$.
- Xavier (compromise): $\sigma_W^2 = 2 / (d_{\text{in}} + d_{\text{out}})$. Use with linear or tanh.
- He (ReLU correction): $\sigma_W^2 = 2 / d_{\text{in}}$. Use with ReLU.
- Biases initialise to zero unless you have prior class-imbalance information.
