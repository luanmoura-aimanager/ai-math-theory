## 1. Why this session exists

Backpropagation is the chain rule from 0.3 applied to the MLP composition from 2.1. To organise the computation we introduce a single variable — the **delta**, $\delta^{(\ell)}$ — that propagates gradient signal backwards through the network. The base case of the backward recursion lives at the loss layer. For softmax + cross-entropy, the answer is the identity we already proved in 1.8.<FN n={1} />

---

## 2. Definition of $\delta^{(\ell)}$

For each layer $\ell$, define

$$
\boxed{\ \delta^{(\ell)} \;:=\; \frac{\partial L}{\partial z^{(\ell)}} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}.\ }
$$

(One column per example in the batched form; we keep working per-example here and re-vectorise in §5.)

In words: $\delta^{(\ell)}$ is the gradient of the loss with respect to the *pre-activation* at layer $\ell$. It is the natural backward-pass variable because once we have it we can compute the gradients w.r.t. $W^{(\ell)}$ and $b^{(\ell)}$ in one step (2.7), *and* we can propagate to $\delta^{(\ell-1)}$ in one step (2.6).

---

## 3. The output delta for softmax + CE

Setup. Final-layer logits $z^{(L)} \in \mathbb{R}^K$, predicted distribution $\hat y = \operatorname{softmax}(z^{(L)}) \in \mathbb{R}^K$, one-hot label $y \in \{0, 1\}^K$, cross-entropy loss

$$
L = -\sum_{k=1}^K y_k\, \log \hat y_k.
$$

This is *exactly* the setup of 1.8. By the result there,

$$
\boxed{\ \delta^{(L)} \;=\; \frac{\partial L}{\partial z^{(L)}} \;=\; \hat y - y.\ }
$$

The "miracle gradient" of the entire chapter. No $\sigma'$ damping, no $K \times K$ matrix — just a $K$-vector residual.

---

## 4. Other common output deltas

Three more output layers we'll need.

**Sigmoid + binary cross-entropy** (the $K = 2$ case, also used per-output for multi-label classification). From 1.2,

$$
\delta^{(L)} = \hat y - y \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

**Linear + mean-squared error** (regression with no output nonlinearity). Loss $L = \tfrac{1}{2} \lVert \hat y - y \rVert^2$ with $\hat y = z^{(L)}$. Then

$$
\delta^{(L)} = \hat y - y \in \mathbb{R}^{d_L}.
$$

Same shape, same expression — the "match the activation to the loss" principle of 1.2 keeps the output delta clean every time.

**Sigmoid + MSE.** This is the *bad* pairing from 1.2 SelfTest (d). It leaves a $\sigma'(z^{(L)})$ multiplier:

$$
\delta^{(L)} = (\hat y - y)\, \odot\, \sigma'(z^{(L)}).
$$

You can do it, but the $\sigma' \in (0, 1/4]$ damping makes confident-but-wrong predictions move slowly. Avoid.

---

## 5. Batched form

For a mini-batch of $B$ examples, define

$$
\Delta^{(\ell)} \;:=\; \frac{\partial L_{\text{batch}}}{\partial Z^{(\ell)}} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}.
$$

Here $L_{\text{batch}} = \tfrac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B L_i$ is the average loss over the batch. By linearity,

$$
\Delta^{(\ell)}_{i, :} \;=\; \frac{1}{B}\, \frac{\partial L_i}{\partial z^{(\ell)}_i} \;=\; \frac{1}{B}\, \delta^{(\ell)}_i.
$$

So the batched $\Delta^{(L)}$ for softmax + CE is

$$
\Delta^{(L)} \;=\; \frac{1}{B}\,(\hat Y - Y) \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{B \times K}.
$$

(In code we usually keep the $1/B$ factor inside the loss reduction. Whether it lives in $\Delta$ or in the final gradient is bookkeeping.)

---

## 6. Sanity check via shapes

- $z^{(L)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_L}$, so $\delta^{(L)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_L}$ ✓.
- $\hat y, y \in \mathbb{R}^{d_L}$, so $\hat y - y \in \mathbb{R}^{d_L}$ ✓.
- Batched: $Z^{(L)}, \hat Y, Y \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_L}$, so $\hat Y - Y \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_L}$ ✓.

All shapes match. This is how we'll spot dimension bugs in 2.6 and 2.7.

---

## 7. Takeaways

- $\delta^{(\ell)} := \partial L / \partial z^{(\ell)}$ is the central backward-pass variable.
- For softmax + CE, $\delta^{(L)} = \hat y - y$ (1.8 reused verbatim).
- Same identity for sigmoid + BCE and for linear + MSE — these are the three "loss-aligned" output heads.
- Avoid sigmoid + MSE: it leaves a $\sigma'$ multiplier and trains slowly.<FN n={2} />
