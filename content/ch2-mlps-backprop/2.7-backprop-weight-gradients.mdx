## 1. Why this session exists

Sessions 2.5 and 2.6 gave us $\delta^{(\ell)}$ at every layer. The last step of backprop is to convert these deltas into the gradients we update with: $\partial L / \partial W^{(\ell)}$ and $\partial L / \partial b^{(\ell)}$. The conversion is one line each, and the pattern is the same "residual × input" outer product we saw in 1.3 and 1.8 — just applied at every layer of the network.<FN n={1} />

---

## 2. Per-example formula

At layer $\ell$, the pre-activation is

$$
z^{(\ell)} \;=\; W^{(\ell)} a^{(\ell-1)} + b^{(\ell)}.
$$

Entry by entry, $z^{(\ell)}_i = \sum_j W^{(\ell)}_{i, j}\, a^{(\ell-1)}_j + b^{(\ell)}_i$. So

$$
\frac{\partial z^{(\ell)}_i}{\partial W^{(\ell)}_{i, j}} = a^{(\ell-1)}_j, \qquad \frac{\partial z^{(\ell)}_p}{\partial W^{(\ell)}_{i, j}} = 0 \text{ for } p \ne i.
$$

By the chain rule with $\delta^{(\ell)} = \partial L / \partial z^{(\ell)}$,

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(\ell)}_{i, j}} \;=\; \sum_p \frac{\partial L}{\partial z^{(\ell)}_p} \cdot \frac{\partial z^{(\ell)}_p}{\partial W^{(\ell)}_{i, j}} \;=\; \delta^{(\ell)}_i \cdot a^{(\ell-1)}_j.
$$

Stacking entries $(i, j)$ into a matrix,

$$
\boxed{\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(\ell)}} \;=\; \delta^{(\ell)}\, \bigl(a^{(\ell-1)}\bigr)^\top \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}}.\ }
$$

Outer product: delta column at layer $\ell$ times activation row at layer $\ell - 1$.

For the bias, $\partial z^{(\ell)}_p / \partial b^{(\ell)}_i = \delta_{pi}$, so

$$
\boxed{\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(\ell)}} \;=\; \delta^{(\ell)} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}.\ }
$$

---

## 3. Batched form

For a mini-batch of $B$ examples, with $\Delta^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_\ell}$ and $A^{(\ell-1)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_{\ell-1}}$ as before, the weight gradient is the sum of per-example outer products, scaled by $1 / B$:

$$
\boxed{\ \frac{\partial L_{\text{batch}}}{\partial W^{(\ell)}} \;=\; \frac{1}{B}\, (\Delta^{(\ell)})^\top\, A^{(\ell-1)} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}}.\ }
$$

(Recover the per-example case by inspecting one row of $\Delta$ and one row of $A$; the matrix product over $B$ rows is the sum of outer products.)

The bias gradient is the column-wise average of $\Delta^{(\ell)}$:

$$
\boxed{\ \frac{\partial L_{\text{batch}}}{\partial b^{(\ell)}} \;=\; \frac{1}{B}\, \sum_{i=1}^B \Delta^{(\ell)}_{i, :} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}.\ }
$$

In PyTorch this is `Delta.T @ A / B` for the weight and `Delta.mean(dim=0)` for the bias.

---

## 4. Shape sanity check

- $W^{(\ell)}$ is $d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}$.
- $\Delta^{(\ell)}$ is $B \times d_\ell$, $A^{(\ell-1)}$ is $B \times d_{\ell-1}$.
- $(\Delta^{(\ell)})^\top A^{(\ell-1)}$ is $d_\ell \times d_{\ell-1}$ ✓ (matches $W^{(\ell)}$).
- $\Delta^{(\ell)}$ summed over rows is $d_\ell$ ✓ (matches $b^{(\ell)}$).

A gradient must be the same shape as the parameter it differentiates. If you ever see a transposed result, you've swapped the order in the outer product.

---

## 5. The full forward + backward picture

End-to-end picture of one training step on a mini-batch.

**Forward (vectorised):** for $\ell = 1, \dots, L$,

$$
Z^{(\ell)} = A^{(\ell-1)} (W^{(\ell)})^\top + b^{(\ell)}, \qquad A^{(\ell)} = g^{(\ell)}(Z^{(\ell)}).
$$

Compute $L_{\text{batch}}$.

**Backward (vectorised):**

1. Initialise $\Delta^{(L)}$ at the output layer (2.5; for softmax + CE it is $\tfrac{1}{B}(\hat Y - Y)$).
2. For $\ell = L - 1, L - 2, \dots, 1$, recurse $\Delta^{(\ell)} = (\Delta^{(\ell+1)} W^{(\ell+1)}) \odot g'^{(\ell)}(Z^{(\ell)})$ (2.6).
3. For each $\ell$, compute $\partial L / \partial W^{(\ell)} = (\Delta^{(\ell)})^\top A^{(\ell-1)}$ and $\partial L / \partial b^{(\ell)} = \Delta^{(\ell)}\, \text{sum over batch axis}$.

(The $1/B$ factor lives wherever you decide; standard practice folds it into the output delta or the loss itself.)

**Update.** For each layer,

$$
W^{(\ell)} \leftarrow W^{(\ell)} - \alpha\, \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(\ell)}}, \qquad b^{(\ell)} \leftarrow b^{(\ell)} - \alpha\, \frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(\ell)}}.
$$

One training step ≈ three forward passes' worth of FLOPs. Convergence is the topic of Chapter 3.

---

## 6. Takeaways

- $\partial L / \partial W^{(\ell)} = \delta^{(\ell)} (a^{(\ell-1)})^\top$. Outer product: delta × input.
- $\partial L / \partial b^{(\ell)} = \delta^{(\ell)}$.
- Batched: $\partial L / \partial W^{(\ell)} = \tfrac{1}{B} (\Delta^{(\ell)})^\top A^{(\ell-1)}$, bias is the batch-mean of $\Delta^{(\ell)}$.
- Combined with 2.5 and 2.6, you now have the complete backprop algorithm.
