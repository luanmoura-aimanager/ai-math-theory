## 1. Why this session exists

Sessions 2.5–2.7 derived the three identities. This one *runs* them. Take a 2-input, 2-hidden, 1-output network with sigmoid activations, pick a single example, and compute every $z$, $a$, $\delta$, $\partial L / \partial W$, $\partial L / \partial b$ by hand. Once the numbers come out right, the abstraction is yours.

This is the kind of exercise to do with a calculator and a notebook the first time, then re-check by writing a 15-line PyTorch script that backprops the same example and matches your numbers to 4 decimals.

---

## 2. The network

Widths $(d_0, d_1, d_2) = (2, 2, 1)$. Sigmoid at both the hidden and output layers. Binary cross-entropy loss. One example.

**Parameters (chosen for clean arithmetic):**

$$
W^{(1)} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.10 & 0.20 \\ 0.30 & 0.40 \end{pmatrix},
\quad b^{(1)} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.00 \\ 0.00 \end{pmatrix},
$$

$$
W^{(2)} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.50 & -0.60 \end{pmatrix},
\quad b^{(2)} = 0.10.
$$

**One example:** $x = (1.0,\, 2.0)$, label $y = 1$.

---

## 3. Forward pass

**Layer 1 pre-activation:**

$$
z^{(1)} = W^{(1)} x + b^{(1)} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.10 \cdot 1 + 0.20 \cdot 2 \\ 0.30 \cdot 1 + 0.40 \cdot 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.50 \\ 1.10 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

**Layer 1 activation:**

$$
a^{(1)} = \sigma(z^{(1)}) = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma(0.50) \\ \sigma(1.10) \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0.6225 \\ 0.7503 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

(Using $\sigma(0.50) = 1 / (1 + e^{-0.50}) \approx 0.6225$, and similarly $\sigma(1.10) \approx 0.7503$.)

**Output pre-activation:**

$$
z^{(2)} = W^{(2)} a^{(1)} + b^{(2)} = 0.50 \cdot 0.6225 + (-0.60) \cdot 0.7503 + 0.10
\approx 0.3113 - 0.4502 + 0.10 = -0.0389.
$$

**Output activation (prediction):**

$$
\hat y = \sigma(z^{(2)}) = \sigma(-0.0389) \approx 0.4903.
$$

**Loss (BCE, label $y = 1$):**

$$
L = -\log \hat y \approx -\log(0.4903) \approx 0.7126.
$$

---

## 4. Backward pass

**Output delta** (sigmoid + BCE, from 2.5):

$$
\delta^{(2)} = \hat y - y \approx 0.4903 - 1 = -0.5097.
$$

**Layer-2 gradients** (a $1 \times 2$ row of weights and a scalar bias, from 2.7):

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(2)}} = \delta^{(2)} (a^{(1)})^\top \approx (-0.5097) \cdot (0.6225,\ 0.7503) \approx (-0.3173,\ -0.3824).
$$

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(2)}} = \delta^{(2)} \approx -0.5097.
$$

**Layer-1 delta** (recursion from 2.6):

$$
\delta^{(1)} = \bigl((W^{(2)})^\top \delta^{(2)}\bigr) \odot \sigma'(z^{(1)}).
$$

First, $(W^{(2)})^\top \delta^{(2)}$:

$$
(W^{(2)})^\top \delta^{(2)} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.50 \\ -0.60 \end{pmatrix} \cdot (-0.5097) \approx \begin{pmatrix} -0.2549 \\ 0.3058 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

And $\sigma'(z^{(1)}) = a^{(1)} \odot (1 - a^{(1)})$:

$$
\sigma'(z^{(1)}) \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0.6225 \cdot 0.3775 \\ 0.7503 \cdot 0.2497 \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0.2350 \\ 0.1874 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

Hadamard product:

$$
\delta^{(1)} \approx \begin{pmatrix} -0.2549 \cdot 0.2350 \\ 0.3058 \cdot 0.1874 \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} -0.0599 \\ 0.0573 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

**Layer-1 gradients:**

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}} = \delta^{(1)} x^\top \approx \begin{pmatrix} -0.0599 \\ 0.0573 \end{pmatrix} \cdot (1.0,\ 2.0)
= \begin{pmatrix} -0.0599 & -0.1198 \\ 0.0573 & 0.1146 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(1)}} = \delta^{(1)} \approx \begin{pmatrix} -0.0599 \\ 0.0573 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

---

## 5. The first SGD update

With learning rate $\alpha = 0.1$,

$$
W^{(1)} \leftarrow W^{(1)} - 0.1 \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}}
\approx \begin{pmatrix} 0.10599 & 0.21198 \\ 0.29427 & 0.38854 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

$$
b^{(1)} \leftarrow b^{(1)} - 0.1 \cdot \delta^{(1)} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0.00599 \\ -0.00573 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

$$
W^{(2)} \leftarrow W^{(2)} - 0.1 \cdot (-0.3173,\ -0.3824) \approx (0.5317,\ -0.5618).
$$

$$
b^{(2)} \leftarrow b^{(2)} - 0.1 \cdot (-0.5097) \approx 0.1510.
$$

## 6. Verification in PyTorch

The following PyTorch snippet should reproduce the numbers above to 4 decimals.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

torch.set_default_dtype(torch.float64)

x = torch.tensor([[1.0, 2.0]])
y = torch.tensor([[1.0]])

W1 = torch.tensor([[0.10, 0.20], [0.30, 0.40]], requires_grad=True)
b1 = torch.tensor([0.0, 0.0], requires_grad=True)
W2 = torch.tensor([[0.50, -0.60]], requires_grad=True)
b2 = torch.tensor([0.10], requires_grad=True)

z1 = x @ W1.T + b1
a1 = torch.sigmoid(z1)
z2 = a1 @ W2.T + b2
y_hat = torch.sigmoid(z2)
L = nn.functional.binary_cross_entropy(y_hat, y)
L.backward()

print("L =", L.item())          # ≈ 0.7126
print("dL/dW2 =", W2.grad)      # ≈ [[-0.3173, -0.3824]]
print("dL/db2 =", b2.grad)      # ≈ -0.5097
print("dL/dW1 =", W1.grad)      # ≈ [[-0.0599, -0.1198], [0.0573, 0.1146]]
print("dL/db1 =", b1.grad)      # ≈ [-0.0599, 0.0573]
```

If your numbers match to four decimals, you've correctly internalised every identity from 2.5–2.7.

## 7. What this exercise teaches

- Backprop is not magic. It is the chain rule applied carefully.
- Each $\delta$ has a clean shape: same as the corresponding $z$.
- Each $\partial L / \partial W$ is an outer product, same shape as the weight.
- Numerical mistakes show up immediately when the PyTorch reference disagrees with your hand-computed values.

---

## 8. Takeaways

- Pick a small net, one example, and run the full forward + backward by hand.
- Match every number to a reference implementation.
- After this exercise, the rest of Chapter 2 (vanishing/exploding, init, autodiff) is layered on a foundation you have actually computed.
