## 1. Why this session exists

Every architecture in Chapter 8 so far is a Transformer with one sub-module modified. State space models (SSMs), and their leading instantiation Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023<FN n={3} />, [arXiv:2312.00752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)), are different: they replace attention entirely with a *recurrent* sequence operator. Per token they need no KV cache, and per generated token they run in $O(d^2)$ time instead of attention's $O(T d)$ (with the cached-K, V matmul). For long-context inference this is a substantial wall-clock advantage.

The question is whether SSMs can match attention on quality at scale. As of 2026 the answer is: *pure* SSMs are competitive but not strictly better than Transformers at the frontier. *Hybrid* architectures (§8.8) that combine SSM and attention layers are an active research direction and have produced models that match dense Transformers at iso-compute. So SSMs matter both as a standalone variant and, more importantly, as the recurrent half of every hybrid model.

This session is brief on purpose. We define the continuous SSM, discretise it to a recurrence, motivate the *selective* (input-dependent) version that became Mamba, count its compute and memory, and place it in the 2026 architectural landscape. The Mamba paper is dense; we will skim its hardware-aware "parallel scan" details and refer the reader to the paper for the kernel-level implementation.

---

## 2. The continuous-time SSM

Fix state dimension $n$ and signal dimension (here we treat single-channel input; multi-channel SSMs are a per-channel collection of single-channel models). A **linear time-invariant state space model** is the continuous-time system

$$
\boxed{\
\begin{aligned}
h'(t) &= A\, h(t) + B\, x(t), \\
y(t)  &= C\, h(t),
\end{aligned}
\ }
$$

with state $h(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$, input $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}$, output $y(t) \in \mathbb{R}$, and matrices $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}, B \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 1}, C \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times n}$. This is the canonical control-theory SSM (Kalman 1960; not specific to deep learning)<FN n={1} />.

The matrix $A$ defines the *intrinsic dynamics* of the hidden state — how it evolves in the absence of input. $B$ defines how new input enters the state. $C$ defines what part of the state is observed as output.

### 2.1 The convolutional view

Solving the ODE (assuming $h(0) = 0$):

$$
h(t) = \int_0^t e^{A(t - s)} B\, x(s)\, ds, \qquad y(t) = \int_0^t \underbrace{C e^{A(t-s)} B}_{=: K(t-s)}\, x(s)\, ds.
$$

So $y$ is the **convolution** of $x$ with the kernel $K(\tau) = C e^{A \tau} B$. The kernel is determined entirely by $(A, B, C)$. This is the operative observation that links SSMs to convolutions and, by Fourier, to fast algorithms.

## 3. Discretisation

To use SSMs on discrete token sequences, we discretise the ODE with step size $\Delta$. The Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) discretisation, which Mamba uses:

$$
\boxed{\
\begin{aligned}
\bar A &= \exp(\Delta A), \\
\bar B &= (\Delta A)^{-1} (\exp(\Delta A) - I) \cdot \Delta B,
\end{aligned}
\ }
$$

so the recurrence becomes

$$
h_t = \bar A\, h_{t-1} + \bar B\, x_t, \qquad y_t = C\, h_t.
$$

This is a *linear recurrent network*. Given the state $h_{t-1}$ and input $x_t$, the new state $h_t$ requires one $n \times n$ matrix-vector multiply (cost $n^2$) plus one $n \times 1$ matrix-vector multiply (cost $n$); the output requires one $1 \times n$ multiply (cost $n$). Per-step cost: $O(n^2)$.

The crucial structural difference from a standard RNN ([5.5](/session/ch5-sequence-modeling/5.5-rnn-bptt)) is that the recurrence is **linear** — no per-step non-linearity inside the state update. Non-linearity is added externally (a SwiGLU-like block applied to $y_t$). Linearity is what enables the parallel-scan formulation of §5.

### 3.1 Structured $A$: from S4 to Mamba

The naive $n \times n$ matrix $A$ has $n^2$ free parameters per channel, which is impractical at the channel counts ($d_\text{model} = 4096$, etc.) of LLM-scale models. The S4 paper (Gu et al., 2022, [arXiv:2111.00396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396))<FN n={2} /> introduced **structured** $A$ matrices — diagonal-plus-low-rank — that have $O(n)$ parameters and admit fast convolutional evaluation via FFTs.

Mamba uses a simpler structured form: $A$ is **diagonal** with negative real entries, so the recurrence is a per-state-coordinate exponential decay:

$$
A = \text{diag}(a_1, \dots, a_n), \quad a_i < 0.
$$

This has only $n$ parameters per channel, and each coordinate of the state evolves independently: $h_t^{(i)} = e^{\Delta a_i} h_{t-1}^{(i)} + (\text{input term})_i$. Different coordinates capture different *timescales* of state retention — small $|a_i|$ means slow decay (long memory), large $|a_i|$ means fast decay (short memory). Empirically, training discovers a spread of timescales without needing explicit constraints.

## 4. Selective SSM: Mamba

The S4 line gave good signal-processing models but lost to Transformers on language tasks. The Mamba paper's diagnosis: a *time-invariant* SSM (with fixed $A, B, C$) cannot do **content-dependent** state updates. For language, the model often needs to decide "keep this token's information in state" vs "forget it" based on the token itself — exactly what attention's content-based addressing does, and what a content-blind SSM cannot.

**Selective SSM** (Mamba's contribution): make $\Delta, B, C$ functions of the input.

$$
\boxed{\
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_t &= \text{softplus}(\text{Linear}_\Delta(x_t)), \\
B_t &= \text{Linear}_B(x_t), \\
C_t &= \text{Linear}_C(x_t).
\end{aligned}
\ }
$$

The recurrence becomes

$$
h_t = \exp(\Delta_t A)\, h_{t-1} + \bar B_t\, x_t, \qquad y_t = C_t\, h_t,
$$

with $\bar B_t$ the input-dependent discretised $B_t$. The matrix $A$ stays time-invariant (so the eigenstructure is preserved); only the *coupling* into and out of state, and the *time step*, vary per token.

The interpretation: $\Delta_t$ controls "how much time has passed" between tokens — large $\Delta_t$ means strong decay of past state (fast forgetting), small $\Delta_t$ means weak decay (long memory). $B_t$ controls how the current token's content enters the state — the model can choose to gate the token in (large $B_t$) or skip it (small $B_t$). $C_t$ controls what part of the state is read out at this step.

This is the SSM analogue of attention's content-addressed gathering: per token, the model dynamically chooses what to remember, how strongly, and what to output.

### 4.1 Why this destroys the convolutional structure

Time-invariant SSMs are convolutions (§2.1), so they can be computed in $O(T \log T)$ via FFT. Selective SSMs are *time-varying* convolutions — the kernel depends on the input — and admit no direct FFT trick.

Mamba's solution is the **parallel scan**: the linear recurrence $h_t = \bar A_t h_{t-1} + \bar B_t x_t$ is an "associative scan" over the sequence (the operator $(h, c) \mapsto \bar A c + h$ is associative when you collect the $\bar A_t, \bar B_t, x_t$ correctly into pairs). Associative scans can be computed in $O(\log T)$ depth with $O(T)$ work using the standard parallel-prefix-sum algorithm. The Mamba paper implements this with a hardware-aware CUDA kernel that minimises HBM↔SRAM transfers, similar in spirit to FlashAttention but for the scan operation.

Per-token training compute: $O(d \cdot n)$ for state update, $O(d \cdot n)$ for output projection — same per-token cost as a Transformer FFN of width $d$ (no quadratic-in-$T$ term, because there is no attention matrix to compute).

## 5. Compute and memory: the headline comparison

Per-layer cost comparison for a single token at position $T$:

| Operator | Train: FLOPs per token | Inference: FLOPs per generated token | KV cache |
|---|---|---|---|
| MHA (with FlashAttention) | $\Theta(T d)$ | $\Theta(T d)$ | $4 d T$ bytes |
| GQA-$8$ | $\Theta(T d)$ | $\Theta(T d)$ | $4 (d/8) T$ bytes |
| MLA | $\Theta(T d)$ | $\Theta(T d + r d)$ | $2 (r + d_h^R) T$ bytes |
| **Selective SSM** | $\Theta(d n)$ | $\Theta(d n)$ | **$0$** (state is $d n$ scalars, fixed not growing) |

The headline: SSM compute per generated token is **constant in $T$**. There is no cache that grows with sequence length. The hidden state has fixed dimension $d \cdot n$, independent of $T$.

This is the structural reason for SSMs' interest in long-context generation:

- At $T = 1{,}000$, a Transformer reads $\sim 10$ MB of KV cache per generated token (LLaMA-7B-class). An SSM reads $\sim 100$ KB of state. The SSM is $\sim 100\times$ faster at the memory-bandwidth level.
- At $T = 100{,}000$, the Transformer reads $\sim 1$ GB per generated token. The SSM still reads $\sim 100$ KB. The SSM is $\sim 10{,}000\times$ faster.
- The crossover point at which an SSM is "free" is essentially at $T = 0$; the constant cost is small enough that SSMs are competitive with attention even at short sequences.

### 5.1 But quality?

The Mamba paper's main empirical claim: at iso-parameters, Mamba matches Transformer perplexity on Pile-style pretraining and outperforms on long-context benchmarks (where the Transformer's effective context degrades). Subsequent work (Mamba-2, Dao & Gu 2024<FN n={4} />; Hymba; etc.) has confirmed and extended this at the 1B–3B scale.

But at the *frontier* ($\sim 100$B+ activated parameters), pure SSMs have not yet matched dense Transformer LLMs on standard benchmarks. The leading hypotheses for the residual gap:

1. **In-context retrieval.** Attention's content-addressed lookup is exact (or nearly so, modulo softmax); an SSM's state is a lossy compression of the past. Tasks that require pulling a specific past token verbatim (e.g., needle-in-haystack) are easier for attention.
2. **Heuristic inductive biases of attention.** Decades of attention-related architectural tuning have given the field a host of small improvements (RoPE, GQA, sliding-window) that SSMs have not yet seen full attention to.
3. **Training stability.** SSMs at very large scale have known numerical-stability issues that the field has not fully worked through.

The current state of the art is to *combine* attention and SSM, which is the subject of §8.8 (hybrids).

<Aside type="note">
**SSM compute vs Transformer compute, at the same $N$.** Both Mamba and a Transformer with FFN+attention spend $\sim 6 N D$ FLOPs to train (the same $6 N D$ rule of [6.12](/session/ch6-attention-transformer/6.12-compute-and-memory)). The compute *budget* is the same; the difference is in *what gets computed*. Transformer: $T^2$-attention plus dense FFN. Mamba: linear-in-$T$ scan plus dense projections. At iso-FLOPs, the two architectures use the parameter budget differently — Mamba has no attention parameters, so all of its parameters go into the SSM state matrices and the residual MLP-like projections.
</Aside>

## 6. The block

A Mamba block, in shape, is

```
x ── RMSNorm ── linear up-proj (to 2*d) ── split ──┐
                                                    │
       ┌── Selective SSM (with selective Δ, B, C) ──┤
       │                                            │
       └── SiLU ──────────── elementwise * ────────┘──── linear down-proj (back to d) ── + (residual) ── x_out
```

In words: pre-norm, up-project to $2d$, split into two halves of width $d$. Apply the selective SSM to one half. Apply SiLU to the other half. Elementwise-multiply (a gating like SwiGLU). Down-project back to $d$. Residual add. There is no separate FFN block: the SSM block already includes its own non-linearity and gating, so a Mamba layer is *one* block (not the two of attention + FFN in a Transformer).

This means a Mamba "layer" at fixed parameter count corresponds to about 2× the number of layers in a Transformer with the same parameters. Mamba-3B has 64 layers; a Transformer-3B has $\sim 32$. The depth doubles but each layer is cheaper.

## 7. What we keep going forward

1. **An SSM is a linear (or near-linear) recurrent operator $h_t = \bar A h_{t-1} + \bar B x_t$, $y_t = C h_t$.** Discretised from a continuous-time linear ODE. With diagonal $A$, the state coordinates evolve at independent timescales.

2. **Selective SSM (Mamba) makes $\Delta, B, C$ input-dependent.** This is the SSM analogue of attention's content-addressed lookup. Time-invariant SSMs (S4) lost to Transformers on language; selective SSMs are competitive.

3. **SSMs have $O(1)$ KV-like state, not $O(T)$.** Decode per token is constant in $T$, vs Transformer's $O(T)$. The wall-clock advantage at long $T$ is enormous.

4. **At iso-parameters, Mamba matches Transformer at $\lesssim 3$B and underperforms slightly at frontier scale.** The leading hypothesis is that exact in-context retrieval needs attention; a lossy state compression does not suffice.

5. **The 2026 frontier uses hybrids.** Interleave Mamba and attention layers (Jamba, Samba) to combine the per-token-throughput advantage of SSMs with the recall accuracy of attention. This is the subject of §8.8.
