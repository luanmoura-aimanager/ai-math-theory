## 1. Why this session exists

[6.6](/session/ch6-attention-transformer/6.6-why-positional-info) ended with the claim: rotate $Q$ and $K$ in 2D planes by angles proportional to position, and the dot product $q_m^\top k_n$ becomes a function of $n - m$ alone. This session makes that claim precise.

The construction has three layers:

1. **2D core.** A single 2D rotation $R(\theta)$ has the property $R(\alpha)^\top R(\beta) = R(\beta - \alpha)$, which is the entire mechanism RoPE uses.
2. **High-dimensional lift.** Split $\mathbb{R}^d$ (with $d$ even) into $d/2$ pairs, each rotated independently at its own frequency.
3. **Frequency schedule.** Pick the per-pair frequencies $\theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d}$ so that the network has access to a wide spectrum of "positional wavelengths" — short ones for local order, long ones for global position.

RoPE is the dominant positional encoding in modern LLMs (LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen, Gemini, and most production LLMs from 2023 onward). The derivation is short and the result has a clean geometric interpretation. Both are below.

---

## 2. The 2D rotation identity

The 2D rotation matrix by angle $\theta$ is

$$
R(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \phantom{-}\cos\theta \end{pmatrix}.
$$

It is **orthogonal**: $R(\theta)^\top R(\theta) = I$. And it has the **composition rule** $R(\alpha) R(\beta) = R(\alpha + \beta)$ — rotations form a one-parameter group. Combining these:

$$
\boxed{\ R(\alpha)^\top R(\beta) = R(-\alpha) R(\beta) = R(\beta - \alpha). \ }
$$

This is the entire identity RoPE uses. The rest is bookkeeping.

### 2.1 Applying to attention

Let $q, k \in \mathbb{R}^2$ be a single query and key (we'll lift to higher dimension in §3). Define the **position-rotated** vectors at positions $m$ and $n$:

$$
\tilde q_m = R(m\theta)\, q, \qquad \tilde k_n = R(n\theta)\, k,
$$

with $\theta$ a fixed "base frequency" (we will pick it in §4). Compute the dot product:

$$
\tilde q_m^\top \tilde k_n = (R(m\theta) q)^\top (R(n\theta) k) = q^\top R(m\theta)^\top R(n\theta) k = q^\top R((n - m)\theta) k.
$$

The dependence on $m$ and $n$ has collapsed into dependence on $(n - m)$ alone. **The dot product of the rotated query and key depends only on their *relative* position.** This is exactly the property [6.6 §4.3](/session/ch6-attention-transformer/6.6-why-positional-info) asked for.

There are no learnable parameters in this construction. The rotations are deterministic functions of $m$ and $n$.

## 3. Lifting to general $d$

A single 2D plane is too narrow for a useful attention head; the standard head dimension is $d_h = 64$ or $128$ (6.4). Lift to general $d$ (even) by **splitting the head dimension into $d / 2$ pairs of coordinates** and rotating each pair independently with its own frequency.

Let $q = (q^{(0)}, q^{(1)}, \dots, q^{(d-1)}) \in \mathbb{R}^d$, and group coordinates as

$$
q = \big(\, (q^{(0)}, q^{(1)}),\ (q^{(2)}, q^{(3)}),\ \dots,\ (q^{(d-2)}, q^{(d-1)})\,\big),
$$

with the $i$-th pair living in the plane spanned by coordinates $(2i, 2i + 1)$ for $i = 0, 1, \dots, d/2 - 1$. Pick $d/2$ frequencies $\theta_0, \theta_1, \dots, \theta_{d/2 - 1}$.

Define the **block-diagonal rotation** at position $m$:

$$
\boxed{\ R_m = \text{block-diag}\!\Big(R(m \theta_0),\ R(m \theta_1),\ \dots,\ R(m \theta_{d/2 - 1})\Big) \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}. \ }
$$

Each $2 \times 2$ block rotates its pair of coordinates by $m \theta_i$. The full matrix is orthogonal and has determinant 1.

The rotated query and key at positions $m$ and $n$ are

$$
\tilde q_m = R_m\, q, \qquad \tilde k_n = R_n\, k.
$$

The dot product expands as a sum over pairs:

$$
\tilde q_m^\top \tilde k_n = \sum_{i = 0}^{d/2 - 1} \big( q^{(2i, 2i+1)} \big)^\top R\!\big((n - m)\theta_i\big)\, k^{(2i, 2i+1)},
$$

where $q^{(2i, 2i+1)}$ denotes the 2D restriction of $q$ to coordinates $(2i, 2i+1)$. Each pair contributes a term that depends only on $n - m$, scaled by the pair's own frequency $\theta_i$.

The full dot product is therefore a sum of $d/2$ "relative-position responses" at different frequencies. This is the key structural property of RoPE: it doesn't encode position as one number per token; it encodes it as a *spectrum* of $d/2$ independent rotations, each contributing to a different range of relative offsets.

## 4. The frequency schedule $\theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d}$

The construction so far works for any choice of $\theta_i$. The choice in the RoPE paper (Su et al., 2021)<FN n={1} /> mimics the sinusoidal positional encoding of AIAYN:

$$
\boxed{\ \theta_i = b^{-2i/d}, \qquad b = 10000,\ i = 0, 1, \dots, d/2 - 1. \ }
$$

The base $b = 10000$ is conventional (and adjustable — long-context fine-tuning often changes it; see 7.13). The resulting frequencies span a wide geometric range:

- **$i = 0$:** $\theta_0 = 1$. Highest frequency. Period $2\pi$ — captures very short-range positional differences ($n - m$ of order 1).
- **$i = d/2 - 1$:** $\theta_{d/2 - 1} = 10000^{-(d-2)/d} \approx 10^{-4}$. Lowest frequency. Period $\approx 2\pi \cdot 10^{4}$ — captures very long-range positional differences.

The spectrum lets the model attend to relative position at multiple "scales." Short-range patterns (n-gram-style) use high-frequency pairs; long-range patterns (document structure) use low-frequency pairs. Different attention heads, and different pairs within a head, end up using different parts of the spectrum.

### 4.1 The connection to sinusoidal encoding

The sinusoidal positional encoding of AIAYN (see [Appendix 6.A](/session/ch6-attention-transformer/6.A-sinusoidal-pe)) sets the $(2i, 2i+1)$ coordinates of $\text{PE}(t)$ to $(\sin(t \theta_i), \cos(t \theta_i))$ with the same $\theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d}$ frequencies. The relation is structural: applying $R(t \theta_i)$ to a 2D vector $v$ rotates it through the same angle that $\sin/\cos$ would sweep. RoPE inherits the frequency schedule but applies it as a *rotation of $Q, K$* rather than as an *additive shift of embeddings*.

## 5. The full RoPE forward pass

Putting the pieces together, RoPE-modified self-attention is:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
Q &= X W_Q, \\
K &= X W_K, \\
V &= X W_V, \\
\tilde Q_m &= R_m\, Q_m \quad \text{(rotate query at position } m \text{)} \\
\tilde K_n &= R_n\, K_n \quad \text{(rotate key at position } n \text{)} \\
A &= \text{softmax}\!\left( \frac{\tilde Q \tilde K^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M \right), \\
Y &= A V.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

Note carefully: **values $V$ are NOT rotated.** RoPE applies *only* to $Q$ and $K$. The rotation's purpose is to make the dot product positional-dependent; the values are then mixed by an attention weight that already carries the position information.

### 5.1 Efficient implementation

The block-diagonal rotation $R_m$ has a clean coordinate form. Pair $i$ at position $m$ acts as:

$$
\begin{pmatrix} \tilde q^{(2i)}_m \\ \tilde q^{(2i+1)}_m \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(m \theta_i) & -\sin(m \theta_i) \\ \sin(m \theta_i) & \phantom{-}\cos(m \theta_i) \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} q^{(2i)} \\ q^{(2i+1)} \end{pmatrix}.
$$

In implementations, this is computed as two element-wise multiplications using pre-computed $\cos$ and $\sin$ tables: with $C_m \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $S_m \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ holding the cosines and sines at position $m$, the rotation is

$$
\tilde Q_m = Q_m \odot C_m + \text{rotate}(Q_m) \odot S_m,
$$

where $\text{rotate}(\cdot)$ swaps adjacent coordinates with a sign flip (the "negate-then-swap" trick). The cost is two element-wise multiplies plus a swap, all $\Theta(T d)$. Trivial compared to the attention dot product.

## 6. Geometric reading

A clean way to picture what RoPE does:

> *"At every position, twist the query and key vectors. The angle of the twist grows linearly with position, and different coordinate pairs are twisted at different rates."*

Two queries at *the same* position have the same twist applied — so RoPE preserves the relative orientation of *queries at the same position*. Two queries at *different* positions are twisted by different angles, so their inner products with a fixed key become position-dependent.

The dot product $\tilde q_m^\top \tilde k_n$ depends only on the *relative twist angle* $(n - m) \theta_i$ in each pair, summed across pairs. The model sees "position" through this spectrum of relative-twist responses, and learns to use the spectrum to express positional patterns of various kinds.

---

## 7. Properties: why this design wins

Five properties make RoPE the modern default:

**(a) Relative-only.** The attention score $\tilde q_m^\top \tilde k_n$ depends only on $n - m$. This is *exactly* what we want from a positional encoding for language.

**(b) No learnable parameters.** The rotations are fixed; the schedule is fixed. No "positional embedding matrix" to size up with context length.

**(c) Decays with distance, controllably.** For each frequency $\theta_i$, the rotation $R((n-m)\theta_i)$ oscillates. Empirically, larger $|n - m|$ tends to produce a smaller mean attention score at random initialisation — a "soft locality prior" that helps training but does not forbid long-range attention.

**(d) Decoupled from values.** Position only affects the *attention weights*, not the values being mixed. The residual stream of [6.10](/session/ch6-attention-transformer/6.10-pre-vs-post-norm) (Part 2) stays clean.

**(e) Tractable extrapolation.** The rotation angles are continuous in position, so the model has *some* signal at positions beyond the training context. Position interpolation, NTK-aware scaling, and YaRN (Peng et al., 2023) all build on this — see 7.13. Compare with learned absolute positional embeddings, which have no defined behaviour at out-of-distribution positions.

<Aside type="note">
**Apply RoPE before the dot product, not in the embedding.** A common confusion: RoPE is *not* added to the input embeddings. It is applied to $Q$ and $K$ *after* their linear projections. This matters: if RoPE were added at the embedding, the FFN block (6.8) would receive positional-modified inputs, and the rotation interpretation would break (rotations don't commute with element-wise non-linearities). Keeping RoPE inside the attention sub-layer is what preserves the clean relative-position structure.
</Aside>

## 8. The base $b = 10000$ and modern long-context training

The base $b$ controls the spread of frequencies. $b = 10000$ was the choice in AIAYN and the original RoPE paper. Two adjustments are common in modern training:

- **Increase $b$ for longer contexts.** LLaMA-2 used $b = 10000$ at 4k context; LLaMA-3 uses $b = 500000$ at 8k context; Qwen-2-72B-1M uses $b \approx 10^7$ at 1M context. Larger $b$ pushes more frequencies into the low-frequency regime, providing finer positional discrimination at longer distances. This is the simplest way to extend context length and is now standard in pretraining.

- **NTK-aware scaling / YaRN.** When *extending* a model's context window without retraining from scratch (e.g. extending a 4k LLaMA to 128k), one can rescale the frequencies $\theta_i$ post hoc by a position-interpolation-like rule. The math is in 7.13.

Both adjustments are downstream of the RoPE construction itself — they are choices of $\{\theta_i\}$, not of the structural form $R_m Q$.

## 9. What we keep going forward

RoPE in one line: **rotate $q$ and $k$ in $d/2$ separate 2D planes by angles proportional to position $m$, with frequencies $\theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d}$.** The dot product becomes a function of $n - m$ alone.

We have now specified every part of the attention sub-layer: similarity (6.2), wiring (6.3), heads (6.4), mask (6.5), and position (6.6, 6.7). 6.8 introduces the FFN sub-layer that completes one Transformer block.
