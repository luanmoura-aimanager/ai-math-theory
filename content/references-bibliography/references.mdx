## Chapter 0 — Math foundations

**Textbooks.**

- **Boyd, S., & Vandenberghe, L.** (2004). *Convex Optimization.* Cambridge University Press. — Used in 0.8 (convex functions, Jensen, first/second-order conditions). [stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/)
- **Cover, T. M., & Thomas, J. A.** (2006). *Elements of Information Theory* (2nd ed.). Wiley. — Used in 0.6 (entropy, KL divergence) and 0.7 (cross-entropy as MLE).
- **Magnus, J. R., & Neudecker, H.** (2019). *Matrix Differential Calculus with Applications in Statistics and Econometrics* (3rd ed.). Wiley. — Standard reference for the numerator-convention matrix calculus of 0.2.
- **Higham, N. J.** (2002). *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms* (2nd ed.). SIAM. — Numerical-stability material in 0.10 (overflow, underflow, log-sum-exp).

**Historical notes / theorems cited.**

- **Rademacher, H.** (1919). "Über partielle und totale Differenzierbarkeit von Funktionen mehrerer Variabeln und über die Transformation der Doppelintegrale". *Math. Annalen*, 79, 340–359. — The almost-everywhere differentiability theorem stated in 0.9.
- **Jensen, J. L. W. V.** (1906). "Sur les fonctions convexes et les inégalités entre les valeurs moyennes". *Acta Math.*, 30, 175–193. — Jensen's inequality (0.8).

**Online resources.**

- **bfloat16 specification.** Wang & Kanwar (2019), "BFloat16: The secret to high performance on Cloud TPUs". [cloud.google.com](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/bfloat16-the-secret-to-high-performance-on-cloud-tpus) — Used in 0.10.

## Chapter 1 — Linear models and the perceptron

**Textbooks.**

- **Bishop, C. M.** (2006). *Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning.* Springer. — Linear regression (1.1), logistic regression (1.2–1.3), softmax (1.7), MAP / Gaussian priors.
- **Hastie, T., Tibshirani, R., & Friedman, J.** (2009). *The Elements of Statistical Learning* (2nd ed.). Springer. — Logistic regression, linear methods.

**Primary papers.**

- **Rosenblatt, F.** (1958). "The perceptron: A probabilistic model for information storage and organization in the brain". *Psychological Review*, 65, 386–408. — Perceptron model (1.5).
- **Novikoff, A. B. J.** (1962). "On convergence proofs on perceptrons". *Symposium on the Mathematical Theory of Automata*, 12, 615–622. — Mistake bound for the perceptron (1.5).
- **Minsky, M., & Papert, S.** (1969). *Perceptrons: An Introduction to Computational Geometry.* MIT Press. — The XOR impossibility result (1.6).

**Connection to dissertation.** Moura, L. M. (2019). *Convolutional neural networks for Raman-spectrum classification*. MSc dissertation, UFC. Appendix C, pp. 155–175 — derivations of logistic regression with cross-entropy, perceptron with step activation, XOR via composition, softmax with multi-class cross-entropy.

## Chapter 2 — MLPs and backpropagation

**Textbooks.**

- **Goodfellow, I., Bengio, Y., & Courville, A.** (2016). *Deep Learning.* MIT Press. — General reference for Chapter 2; specifically §6 (deep feed-forward networks), §8 (optimization), §6.4 (UAT). [deeplearningbook.org](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/)
- **Griewank, A., & Walther, A.** (2008). *Evaluating Derivatives: Principles and Techniques of Algorithmic Differentiation* (2nd ed.). SIAM. — Canonical AD reference (2.11).

**Primary papers.**

- **Cybenko, G.** (1989). "Approximation by superpositions of a sigmoidal function". *Mathematics of Control, Signals, and Systems*, 2(4), 303–314. — UAT for sigmoidal activations (2.2).
- **Hornik, K.** (1991). "Approximation capabilities of multilayer feedforward networks". *Neural Networks*, 4(2), 251–257. — UAT for general non-polynomial activations (2.2).
- **Telgarsky, M.** (2016). "Benefits of depth in neural networks". *COLT*. [arXiv:1602.04485](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04485) — Depth-vs-width separation (2.2).
- **Eldan, R., & Shamir, O.** (2016). "The power of depth for feedforward neural networks". *COLT*. [arXiv:1512.03965](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03965) — Depth-2 vs depth-3 separation (2.2).
- **Rumelhart, D. E., Hinton, G. E., & Williams, R. J.** (1986). "Learning representations by back-propagating errors". *Nature*, 323(6088), 533–536. — Backpropagation (2.5–2.7).
- **Baur, W., & Strassen, V.** (1983). "The complexity of partial derivatives". *Theoretical Computer Science*, 22(3), 317–330. — Cost-of-gradient = cost-of-function theorem (2.11).
- **Hochreiter, S.** (1991). *Untersuchungen zu dynamischen neuronalen Netzen.* Diploma thesis, TU Munich. — First vanishing-gradient analysis in RNNs (2.9).
- **Bengio, Y., Simard, P., & Frasconi, P.** (1994). "Learning long-term dependencies with gradient descent is difficult". *IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks*, 5(2), 157–166. — Formal vanishing-gradient analysis (2.9).
- **Glorot, X., & Bengio, Y.** (2010). "Understanding the difficulty of training deep feedforward neural networks". *AISTATS*. — Xavier/Glorot initialisation (2.10).
- **He, K., Zhang, X., Ren, S., & Sun, J.** (2015). "Delving deep into rectifiers: Surpassing human-level performance on ImageNet classification". *ICCV*. [arXiv:1502.01852](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.01852) — He / Kaiming initialisation (2.10).
- **Krizhevsky, A., Sutskever, I., & Hinton, G. E.** (2012). "ImageNet classification with deep convolutional neural networks". *NeurIPS*. — AlexNet; popularised ReLU at scale (2.3).
- **Hendrycks, D., & Gimpel, K.** (2016). "Gaussian Error Linear Units (GELUs)". [arXiv:1606.08415](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.08415) — GELU activation (2.3).
- **Ramachandran, P., Zoph, B., & Le, Q. V.** (2017). "Searching for activation functions". [arXiv:1710.05941](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05941) — Swish / SiLU (2.3).
- **Shazeer, N.** (2020). "GLU variants improve Transformer". [arXiv:2002.05202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05202) — SwiGLU, GeGLU (2.3, will reappear in Cap 8).

**Implementation references.**

- **Karpathy, A.** "Neural Networks: Zero to Hero" lecture series, especially *micrograd* (Lecture 1). [karpathy.ai/zero-to-hero](https://karpathy.ai/zero-to-hero.html) — Used as resource in 2.12.

## Chapter 3 — Optimization and regularization

**Primary papers — optimisers.**

- **Duchi, J., Hazan, E., & Singer, Y.** (2011). "Adaptive subgradient methods for online learning and stochastic optimization". *JMLR*, 12, 2121–2159. — AdaGrad (3.4).

## Citation conventions used in this course

- **Inline citations** use author–year format, e.g. *(Vaswani et al., 2017)*.
- **arXiv references** are linked directly to the abstract page.
- **Books** are cited by author, year, title, and edition; chapter references where relevant.
- **Lecture series** include the lecturer, course name, and a stable URL.
- **Anthropic / OpenAI / Google AI blog posts** are cited with author (or "Anthropic", etc.), title, year, and URL.

This bibliography is the source of truth — if a session body cites a paper inline, that paper is listed here. If you find a citation that's missing from this page, file a fix.
