## Chapter 0 — Math foundations

**Textbooks.**

- **Boyd, S., & Vandenberghe, L.** (2004). *Convex Optimization.* Cambridge University Press. — Used in 0.8 (convex functions, Jensen, first/second-order conditions). [stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/)
- **Cover, T. M., & Thomas, J. A.** (2006). *Elements of Information Theory* (2nd ed.). Wiley. — Used in 0.6 (entropy, KL divergence) and 0.7 (cross-entropy as MLE).
- **Magnus, J. R., & Neudecker, H.** (2019). *Matrix Differential Calculus with Applications in Statistics and Econometrics* (3rd ed.). Wiley. — Standard reference for the numerator-convention matrix calculus of 0.2.
- **Higham, N. J.** (2002). *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms* (2nd ed.). SIAM. — Numerical-stability material in 0.10 (overflow, underflow, log-sum-exp).

**Historical notes / theorems cited.**

- **Rademacher, H.** (1919). "Über partielle und totale Differenzierbarkeit von Funktionen mehrerer Variabeln und über die Transformation der Doppelintegrale". *Math. Annalen*, 79, 340–359. — The almost-everywhere differentiability theorem stated in 0.9.
- **Jensen, J. L. W. V.** (1906). "Sur les fonctions convexes et les inégalités entre les valeurs moyennes". *Acta Math.*, 30, 175–193. — Jensen's inequality (0.8).

**Online resources.**

- **bfloat16 specification.** Wang & Kanwar (2019), "BFloat16: The secret to high performance on Cloud TPUs". [cloud.google.com](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/bfloat16-the-secret-to-high-performance-on-cloud-tpus) — Used in 0.10.

**Primary papers — CNNs.**

- **Hubel, D. H., & Wiesel, T. N.** (1962). "Receptive fields, binocular interaction and functional architecture in the cat's visual cortex". *Journal of Physiology*, 160, 106–154. — Biological origin of local receptive fields (4.2).

- **Krizhevsky, A., Sutskever, I., & Hinton, G. E.** (2012). "ImageNet classification with deep convolutional neural networks". *NeurIPS*. — AlexNet; also cited in Cap 2 §2.3.
- **Luo, W., Li, Y., Urtasun, R., & Zemel, R.** (2016). "Understanding the effective receptive field in deep convolutional neural networks". *NeurIPS*. [arXiv:1701.04128](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04128) — Effective vs theoretical receptive field; Gaussian shape (4.2 §5.3).
- **He, K., Zhang, X., Ren, S., & Sun, J.** (2016). "Deep residual learning for image recognition". *CVPR*. [arXiv:1512.03385](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385) — ResNet; deep stacks of small kernels (referenced in 4.2 §5).

**Primary papers — convolutional sequence models.**

- **van den Oord, A., Dieleman, S., Zen, H., et al.** (2016). "WaveNet: A generative model for raw audio". [arXiv:1609.03499](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03499) — Dilated 1D convolutions for sequences (4.2 Aside, 4.3 §2).
- **Bai, S., Kolter, J. Z., & Koltun, V.** (2018). "An empirical evaluation of generic convolutional and recurrent networks for sequence modeling". [arXiv:1803.01271](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01271) — Temporal convolutional networks; competitive with RNNs but limited at long range (4.3 §2).

**Implementation references.**

- **Dumoulin, V., & Visin, F.** (2016). "A guide to convolution arithmetic for deep learning". [arXiv:1603.07285](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.07285) — Definitive reference for padding, stride, dilation, and transposed convolutions (Appendices 4.A, 4.C).

**Textbooks.**

- **Goodfellow, I., Bengio, Y., & Courville, A.** (2016). *Deep Learning,* §10 (Sequence Modeling: Recurrent and Recursive Nets). MIT Press. — RNN, BPTT, LSTM/GRU (5.5, Appendix 5.A).

- **Radford, A., Wu, J., Child, R., et al.** (2019). "Language models are unsupervised multitask learners". OpenAI technical report. — GPT-2 and the introduction of byte-level BPE (5.1 §5).

**Primary papers — embeddings.**

- **Harris, Z. S.** (1954). "Distributional structure". *Word,* 10(2–3), 146–162. — Distributional hypothesis (5.2 §3).
- **Bengio, Y., Ducharme, R., Vincent, P., & Jauvin, C.** (2003). "A neural probabilistic language model". *JMLR,* 3, 1137–1155. — First neural LM with learned word embeddings; the conceptual ancestor of every modern LLM (5.2, 5.4).

- **Press, O., & Wolf, L.** (2017). "Using the output embedding to improve language models". *EACL*. [arXiv:1608.05859](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05859) — Weight tying between input embedding and output projection (5.2 §6.1).

- **Hochreiter, S.** (1991). *Untersuchungen zu dynamischen neuronalen Netzen.* Diploma thesis, TU Munich. — First analysis of vanishing gradients in RNNs (already cited in Cap 2; recalled in 5.5).
- **Bengio, Y., Simard, P., & Frasconi, P.** (1994). "Learning long-term dependencies with gradient descent is difficult". *IEEE Trans. Neural Networks,* 5(2), 157–166. — Formal vanishing/exploding gradient theorem for RNNs (5.5 §4.1).

## Chapter 6 — Attention and the Transformer

**Textbooks.**

- **Prince, S. J. D.** (2023). *Understanding Deep Learning,* §12 (Sequence-to-Sequence Models) and §13 (Transformers). MIT Press. — Attention as soft lookup (6.1), scaled dot-product attention (6.2), multi-head attention (6.4).
- **Raschka, S.** (2024). *Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch),* Chapters 3–4. Manning. — Self-attention implementation (6.3, 6.4), causal masking (6.5), pre-norm block (6.10–6.11).
- **Bishop, C. M., & Bishop, H.** (2024). *Deep Learning: Foundations and Concepts,* §12 (Transformers). Springer. — Concise modern treatment of all attention components.

**Primary papers — core attention and Transformer.**

- **Vaswani, A., Shazeer, N., Parmar, N., et al.** (2017). "Attention is all you need". *NeurIPS*. [arXiv:1706.03762](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) — The Transformer paper. Scaled dot-product attention (6.2), multi-head attention (6.4), encoder–decoder cross-attention (6.3), sinusoidal positional encoding (Appendix 6.A), the FFN sub-layer (6.8), post-LN block diagram (6.10), full architecture read (6.11).
- **Bahdanau, D., Cho, K., & Bengio, Y.** (2014). "Neural machine translation by jointly learning to align and translate". [arXiv:1409.0473](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473) — First "neural attention" paper; additive attention as a precursor to scaled dot-product (6.1, 6.2).
- **Luong, M.-T., Pham, H., & Manning, C. D.** (2015). "Effective approaches to attention-based neural machine translation". *EMNLP*. [arXiv:1508.04025](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04025) — Dot-product attention variants (6.2).
- **Radford, A., Narasimhan, K., Salimans, T., & Sutskever, I.** (2018). "Improving language understanding by generative pre-training" (GPT-1). OpenAI technical report. — Decoder-only Transformer with causal self-attention (6.3, 6.5); first use of learned PE (Appendix 6.B).
- **Radford, A., Wu, J., Child, R., et al.** (2019). "Language models are unsupervised multitask learners" (GPT-2). OpenAI technical report. — Learned absolute positional encoding (Appendix 6.B), pre-LN variant (6.10), the architectural skeleton of every modern decoder-only LLM (6.11).

**Primary papers — positional encodings.**

- **Shaw, P., Uszkoreit, J., & Vaswani, A.** (2018). "Self-attention with relative position representations". *NAACL*. [arXiv:1803.02155](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02155) — First relative positional encoding (6.6 §4.2).
- **Raffel, C., Shazeer, N., Roberts, A., et al.** (2020). "Exploring the limits of transfer learning with a unified text-to-text transformer" (T5). *JMLR*. [arXiv:1910.10683](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683) — Bucketed relative positional bias (6.6 §4.2).
- **Su, J., Lu, Y., Pan, S., et al.** (2021). "RoFormer: Enhanced transformer with rotary position embedding". [arXiv:2104.09864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864) — Rotary positional embedding (RoPE) (6.7).
- **Press, O., Smith, N. A., & Lewis, M.** (2021). "Train short, test long: Attention with linear biases enables input length extrapolation" (ALiBi). [arXiv:2108.12409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409) — Linear-distance bias inside softmax; length extrapolation (6.13 §3).
- **Chen, S., Wong, S., Chen, L., & Tian, Y.** (2023). "Extending context window of large language models via positional interpolation". [arXiv:2306.15595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595) — Linear interpolation of RoPE frequencies for context extension (6.13 §4.1).
- **Peng, B., Quesnelle, J., Fan, H., & Shippole, E.** (2023). "YaRN: Efficient context window extension of large language models". [arXiv:2309.00071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071) — Piecewise RoPE scaling (NTK-aware + linear-interpolation hybrid) (6.13 §4.3).

**Primary papers — residual connections and normalisation placement.**

- **He, K., Zhang, X., Ren, S., & Sun, J.** (2015). "Deep residual learning for image recognition" (ResNet). [arXiv:1512.03385](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385) — Identity skip connections; the architectural innovation imported wholesale into the Transformer (6.9).
- **He, K., Zhang, X., Ren, S., & Sun, J.** (2016). "Identity mappings in deep residual networks". [arXiv:1603.05027](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05027) — Pre-activation ResNet; the structural blueprint behind pre-LN Transformer (6.10 §6).
- **Veit, A., Wilber, M. J., & Belongie, S.** (2016). "Residual networks behave like ensembles of relatively shallow networks". [arXiv:1605.06431](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06431) — Implicit-ensemble view of residual networks (6.9 Aside).

## Citation conventions used in this course

- **Inline citations** use author–year format, e.g. *(Vaswani et al., 2017)*.
- **arXiv references** are linked directly to the abstract page.
- **Books** are cited by author, year, title, and edition; chapter references where relevant.
- **Lecture series** include the lecturer, course name, and a stable URL.
- **Anthropic / OpenAI / Google AI blog posts** are cited with author (or "Anthropic", etc.), title, year, and URL.
