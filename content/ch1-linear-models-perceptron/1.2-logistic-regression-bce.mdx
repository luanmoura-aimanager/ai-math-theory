## 1. Why this session exists

Linear regression handled $y \in \mathbb{R}$. For binary classification we need $y \in \{0, 1\}$ and a model that outputs a probability $\hat y \in (0, 1)$. The **sigmoid** is the canonical bridge: it squashes $\mathbb{R}$ into $(0, 1)$ in the way you want to interpret $\hat y$ as a Bernoulli probability. Maximum likelihood for that Bernoulli model produces **binary cross-entropy** (BCE), and the gradient of BCE with respect to the pre-activation has a famously clean form — $\hat y - y$. This is the template every later session in the course will follow.

---

## 2. The sigmoid

Define the **sigmoid** function

$$
\sigma : \mathbb{R} \to (0, 1), \qquad \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}.
$$

Three properties to internalise.

**Range and monotonicity.** $\sigma$ is strictly increasing from $\sigma(-\infty) = 0$ to $\sigma(+\infty) = 1$, with $\sigma(0) = 1/2$.

**Symmetry.** $1 - \sigma(z) = \sigma(-z)$. (Multiply numerator and denominator of $\sigma(-z)$ by $e^z$ to verify.)

**Self-derivative identity.** Differentiate

$$
\sigma'(z) = \frac{d}{dz} \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}} = \frac{e^{-z}}{(1 + e^{-z})^2}
= \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}} \cdot \frac{e^{-z}}{1 + e^{-z}}.
$$

The first factor is $\sigma(z)$. The second is $\sigma(-z) = 1 - \sigma(z)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\ \sigma'(z) \;=\; \sigma(z)\,\bigl(1 - \sigma(z)\bigr).\ }
$$

The derivative is expressed in terms of the function value itself — a property that will save us repeated work during backprop.

## 3. The Bernoulli model for binary classification

Let $y \in \{0, 1\}$ and define the **logit**

$$
z = w^\top x + b.
$$

Model the conditional probability of class $1$ by the sigmoid of the logit:

$$
\hat y \;=\; p(y = 1 \mid x; w, b) \;=\; \sigma(z), \qquad p(y = 0 \mid x) = 1 - \hat y.
$$

A compact single expression covering both classes:

$$
p(y \mid x; w, b) = \hat y^{\,y}\,(1 - \hat y)^{1 - y}.
$$

(Plug $y = 0$ and $y = 1$ to recover the two Bernoulli probabilities.)

---

## 4. From MLE to binary cross-entropy

Given i.i.d. data $\{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^m$, the log-likelihood is

$$
\log p(y_{1:m} \mid X; w, b) = \sum_{i=1}^m \bigl[\,y_i \log \hat y_i + (1 - y_i) \log (1 - \hat y_i)\,\bigr],
$$

with $\hat y_i = \sigma(w^\top x_i + b)$. MLE maximises this. Negating to convert to a minimisation,

$$
\boxed{\ L_{\text{BCE}}(w, b) \;=\; -\frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=1}^m \bigl[\,y_i \log \hat y_i + (1 - y_i) \log(1 - \hat y_i)\,\bigr].\ }
$$

This is **binary cross-entropy**, the negative average log-likelihood of the Bernoulli model. Same recipe as 0.7 (cross-entropy = NLL) and 1.1 (least squares = NLL); only the family of distributions changed.

---

## 5. The clean gradient: $\partial L / \partial z = \hat y - y$

Work on a single example, so drop the average and the index $i$. Let $L = -y \log \hat y - (1 - y) \log(1 - \hat y)$ with $\hat y = \sigma(z)$. We compute $\partial L / \partial z$ via the chain rule

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \hat y} \cdot \frac{\partial \hat y}{\partial z}.
$$

Each factor in turn.

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \hat y}
= -\frac{y}{\hat y} + \frac{1 - y}{1 - \hat y}
= \frac{-y(1 - \hat y) + (1 - y)\hat y}{\hat y (1 - \hat y)}
= \frac{\hat y - y}{\hat y (1 - \hat y)}.
$$

From §2,

$$
\frac{\partial \hat y}{\partial z} = \sigma'(z) = \hat y\,(1 - \hat y).
$$

Multiplying,

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial z} = \frac{\hat y - y}{\hat y (1 - \hat y)} \cdot \hat y (1 - \hat y) = \hat y - y.
$$

$$
\boxed{\ \frac{\partial L_{\text{BCE}}}{\partial z} \;=\; \hat y - y.\ }
$$

The $\hat y (1 - \hat y)$ in the denominator of the first factor cancels exactly with the sigmoid derivative — the same cancellation we'll see again with softmax + cross-entropy in 1.8. **This is the reason we pair sigmoid with BCE** (and softmax with CE): the gradient at the pre-activation has no $\sigma'$ tail to amplify or shrink. Compare with pairing sigmoid + MSE, which leaves a $\sigma' = \sigma(1 - \sigma) \in (0, 1/4]$ multiplier and creates a vanishing-gradient pathology when the model is confident.

## 6. The decision rule

At inference, the sigmoid model predicts class $1$ when $\hat y > 1/2$, equivalently $z > 0$, equivalently $w^\top x + b > 0$. The boundary is the hyperplane $w^\top x + b = 0$. The classifier's class decisions depend only on the **sign** of the logit; the magnitude controls confidence.

This is the geometric handle we'll formalise in 1.4 (separating hyperplane, margin) and re-use throughout Chapters 1–6.

---

## 7. Takeaways

- Sigmoid: smooth, monotone, $(0,1)$-valued, with self-derivative $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z))$.
- Sigmoid + Bernoulli MLE = BCE loss.
- $\partial L / \partial z = \hat y - y$. Exact, simple, and the reason this is the canonical pairing.
